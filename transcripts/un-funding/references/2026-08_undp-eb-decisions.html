# UNDP Executive Board decisions — August 2026 session

**Source:** UNDP Executive Board, Second regular session 2026. Decisions adopted at the session covering the meetings in this track (24-26 August 2026).

## Decision 2026/26 — Engagement with the UN80 initiative

The Executive Board:

1. Takes note of the updates provided by UNDP, UNFPA and UNOPS on the UN80 initiative and its implementation; 2. Also takes note of the additional information provided pursuant to decision 2026/16 in relation to the UNDP/UNOPS merger assessment and the UNFPA/United Nations Entity for Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women (UN-Women) merger assessment, including information on alternative options; 3. Stresses that entity-specific merger discussions should not be considered in isolation from the broader UN80 reform work, including, inter alia, United Nations country team reconfiguration, the regional reset, the expertise-on-demand mechanism and funding mechanisms, as they directly impact the entities' ability to deliver on their mandates at the country level; 4. Requests that outstanding legal advice is provided to the board immediately and the unredacted final packages are also shared; 5. Recalls decision 2026/2, as reiterated in decision 2026/16, in which it emphasized that documentation related to the UN80 initiative and its implementation should be shared with the Executive Board as soon as it becomes available, and no later than three weeks prior to any formal or informal session, to allow sufficient time for its consideration, and stresses the importance of full and consistent adherence to this requirement; 6. Recalls General Assembly resolutions 48/162 and 50/227, and reaffirms the Executive Board's responsibility, within its mandate, to consider and recommend any proposed structural changes, to United Nations entities under its governance, while following the existing legal framework, as well as established procedural steps, and in this regard to issue a recommendation on the potential merger or alternative options, through a decision to be transmitted to the Economic and Social Council and through the Council to the General Assembly as necessary before a decision on a potential merger is scheduled for consideration by the General Assembly; 7. Recalls General Assembly resolutions 79/318 and 80/155, and reaffirms the central role of Member States in the reform process, which should be inclusive, evidence-based and transparent, and in this regard calls on UNDP, UNFPA and UNOPS to actively engage with members and observers of the Executive Board;

### With regard to UNFPA

8. Strongly emphasizes that any structural reform must fully safeguard the mandate of UNFPA and demonstrably strengthen country-level delivery; 9. Takes note of the Internal UNFPA legal advice, which states that the consideration at the level of the General Assembly of a potential merger opens the possibility and creates an inherent risk that the mandate of UNFPA could be subject to reconsideration as a part of that process; 10. Also takes note of the additional information, analysis and alternative options submitted by UNFPA pursuant to paragraph 14 of decision 2026/16, including preliminary financial analysis; 11. Decides that the Executive Board, while remaining committed to engaging constructively with the UN80 initiative, is not in a position to make a recommendation of support on a potential merger between UNFPA and UN-Women at this time and at least not before the next formal meeting of the Executive Board, acknowledging the associated risks to safeguarding the mandates and continuity to country level delivery, including the importance of ensuring coherence and alignment among ongoing reforms; 12. Encourages UNFPA to explore implementation of alternative options, within its authority, including enhanced governance coordination and country-level delivery, to enhance system-wide coherence and efficiency, without prejudice to a possible later recommendation by the Executive Board on the proposed merger, and requests that the Executive Board be kept informed; 13. Requests the bureau in collaboration with the bureau of UN-Women to explore possible options to strengthen future Executive Boards engagement and deliberation on this matter;

### With regard to UNDP-UNOPS

14. Concludes that the Executive Board is not yet in a position to make a conclusive recommendation on a potential merger between UNDP and UNOPS at this time, as the necessary due diligence has yet to be completed and appreciates time to further analyse outstanding information; 15. Takes note of the revised terms of reference for the independent assessment and requests that they be further considered through informal consultations to address any outstanding elements, including an estimated budget, as soon as possible but not later than 15 September 2026, with input from UNDP and UNOPS as appropriate; and, pending the conclusion of this process, encourages UNDP and UNOPS to continue working collaboratively on those elements requested in decision 2026/16 that can be advanced jointly by the two organizations, without prejudice to the finalization and approval of the terms of reference; 16. Requests the final terms of reference to be submitted for consideration for approval and an estimated budget be communicated before the end of September 2026 to the Executive Board through a written procedure in line with the existing rules of procedure of the Executive Board; 17. Emphasizes that the envisioned potential merger operational models, or alternatives, in the independent assessment should include worked illustrations of how they would operate in practice at the country level, in a representative sample of country settings, within the established scope set out in decision 2026/16; and encourages any next steps, including any final report for the Executive Board's considerations, to be clear and actionable to support the Executive Board's review; 18. Further requests UNDP and UNOPS, once the terms of reference have been approved, to submit a progress report at each session of the Executive Board during the assessment period, and the completed assessment, together with their joint management response and the independent advice requested in paragraph 15 of decision 2026/16, to the Executive Board sufficiently in advance of its consideration of the matter, and at the latest six weeks before the annual session of the Executive Board in 2027; 19. Requests UNDP and UNOPS to continue exploring implementation of measures to enhance system-wide coherence, coordination and efficiency, including through enhanced operational alignment and streamlining, enhanced coordination and enabling mechanisms at senior leadership level, and administrative cost saving measures such as the expanded use of shared global service centres, and common back offices as well as developing joint offers, and requests them to jointly present progress and updates to the Executive Board under existing reporting mechanisms on the UN80 initiative; 20. Emphasizes that the review of the information submitted and the approval of the terms of reference and associated budget estimate do not prejudge the Executive Board's future consideration of, or recommendation on, any potential merger or alternative option; 21. Reiterates its request in decision 2026/2 to include an agenda item for decision and to take stock of progress on the UN80 initiative at each Executive Board session in 2027, to be provided in the form of an information note on UNDP/UNFPA/UNOPS engagement across the UN80 workstream three's work packages, providing analysis on impact at the country, regional and global levels and identifying emerging risks and resource implications, and requests that sufficient time be allocated for the consideration of UN80 reform proposals.

## Decision 2026/27 — Structured dialogue on financing the results of the UNDP Strategic Plan

The Executive Board, with regard to the structured dialogue on financing the results of the UNDP Strategic Plan (DP/2026/26):

1. Takes note of the report on the structured dialogue on financing the results of the UNDP Strategic Plan (DP/2026/26), and its annexes, including progress made on the implementation of entity-specific commitments under the United Nations funding compact; 2. Notes the importance of sufficient and predictable regular (core) and quality resources, including multi-year, flexible thematic and pooled funding, linked to intended and demonstrated results, as these are critical for UNDP to deliver on its mandate and achieve the results of its Strategic Plan; 3. Encourages all Member States to continue striving towards the shared commitments of the funding compact, noting its voluntary nature, including by maintaining and, where possible, enhancing predictable and flexible funding, including regular, multi-year and thematic contributions, and to disburse funding in a timely manner to support implementation of the Strategic Plan; 4. Encourages UNDP to continue its engagement with Member States through structured funding dialogues, in order to prioritize contributions to regular resources and to promote a shift from highly earmarked to more predictable and quality funding, in line with the funding compact commitments; 5. Encourages UNDP, in light of the decline in the share of regular resources, to explore innovative options to strengthen and diversify its regular resources contributor base, as well as other options to safeguard core capacities, and address the imbalance between core and non-core resources and to report on this to the Executive Board; 6. Recalls its decisions 2024/24 and 2025/25, in which it requested UNDP, in collaboration with UNFPA, UNICEF and UN-Women, to include information on challenges that hinder full implementation of the joint cost-recovery policy in subsequent joint harmonized reports, and notes the updates provided in this regard; 7. Further encourages UNDP to continue engaging Member States and other contributing partners to implement the cost-recovery policy, and to ensure that regular resources do not subsidize activities financed from non-core resources; 8. Encourages UNDP to continue engaging with relevant stakeholders, including the private sector, foundations, civil society, individuals, and international financial institutions, to diversify its potential sources of funding and strengthen partnerships; 9. Encourages UNDP to keep the Executive Board more regularly informed, including through informal briefings on organizational culture prior to the first regular session of the Executive Board, on workforce optimization efforts and related measures to adapt its global footprint, particularly in response to the continued decline in core funding, and their implications for implementation of UNDP's strategic plan, including in programme countries;

### With regard to the host country contributions to UNDP local presence office costs

10. Takes note of the report on host country contributions to UNDP local presence office costs and the update it provides on the status of programme countries contributions, which have supported UNDP's local presence over many years; 11. Reaffirms regulation 9.01 of UNDP financial regulations and rules and endorses the proposed way forward on Government Local Office Costs Plus (GLOC+) in Annex IV to DP/2026/26, report on host country contributions to UNDP local presence office costs, namely, affirming the more current definition of GLOC+ under Financial Regulation 9.01 for the purpose of establishing GLOC targets, encouraging the inclusion of GLOC+ commitments in country programme documents and approving a one-time, time-bound incentive for collection that allows cash payments received against prior period LIC/MIC GLOC arrears incurred up to 31 December 2024, and as applicable, UMIC/NCC unpaid physical presence contributions to be used for in-country programme planning and development effectiveness activities; with the understanding that the inclusion of GLOC+ figures shall be subject to the consent of the host government and shall not affect the formulation of a country programme document or create any financial liability for the programme country, and requests the Administrator to report to the Executive Board, through existing reporting mechanisms, on its implementation;

### With regard to the annual review of the financial situation of the United Nations Capital Development Fund, 2025 (DP/2026/27)

12. Takes note of the annual review of the financial situation of the United Nations Capital Development Fund, 2025 (DP/2026/27).
